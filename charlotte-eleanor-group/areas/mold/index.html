Prices, up front

What it costs, before you ask.

Most salons around Mold make you ring for a price. We would rather you saw it here. These are starting prices for an appointment in your own home — there is no call-out fee and no mileage charge anywhere on the round.

Every price here is a “from” price. What you actually pay depends on how long and how thick your hair is — the booking form asks, works it out and shows you the figure before you confirm. That figure does not change at the door. In detail: cuts & styling, colour & balayage, perms & treatments, bridal.

The round

Mold, and every village around it.

Mold — Yr Wyddgrug — is the old county town, and the CH7 postcode fans out from it in every direction. Most of those villages have no salon of their own, and the ones that do have one are a drive away up a single-track lane. That is exactly the shape of place a mobile service is built for.

West

The Alyn valley

Gwernaffield (Y Waun), Pantymwyn, Cilcain, Rhydymwyn, Nannerch, Caerwys, Afonwen. Lanes climbing towards Moel Famau and the Clwydian Range — beautiful to live in, tedious to drive out of for a blow-dry. We come up instead.

North-west

Halkyn Mountain

Rhosesmor, Rhes-y-cae, Wern y Gaer, Halkyn, Pentre Halkyn, Brynford. Scattered limestone villages on the mountain road, with almost nothing between them and a long way to anywhere with a salon chair in it.

North & east

Northop & Sychdyn

Sychdyn, Northop (Llaneurgain), Northop Hall, Mynydd Isa, New Brighton, Bryn-y-Baal, Alltami. The busiest corner of the round — and the one where an appointment at home saves a run onto the A55.

East

Buckley & Drury

Buckley, Drury, Padeswood. The largest settlements on the round and only two miles from Mold — close enough that a household booking on one street tends to fill the rest of the morning.

South

Nercwys to Leeswood

Gwernymynydd, Nercwys, Treuddyn, Leeswood (Coed-llai), Coed-talon, Pontblyddyn. Small villages, long-standing clients, and the kind of round where word travels fast.

Over the tops

Llanferres & the Alun

Llanferres, Loggerheads, Cadole, Maeshafn, Eryrys, Llanarmon-yn-Iâl. Ten minutes out of town on the A494 and routinely missed by anyone working to a council boundary rather than a postcode.

The whole round

Every lane, not
just the main road.

Thirty-six villages, and the van is in a different corner of them each working day rather than criss-crossing the lot. That is the whole reason a village booking holds: your slot is offered on the day the van is already in your valley, not squeezed into a gap between two runs across the tops. Tell us your village when you book and the diary does the rest.

On the day

What actually happens.

Your stylist arrives at the time booked in a fully-kitted electric van — professional tools, a portable inclinable wash station with its own water tanks, and salon products. She sets up wherever suits you: the kitchen, the conservatory, in front of a mirror upstairs if that is easier.

Then it is the same consultation, the same craft and the same finish you would get in the chair. Afterwards everything is packed away, the floor is left as it was found, you tap the card machine and we rebook you before we leave.

  • All we need — a chair near a plug socket and ordinary tap water
  • Deposits & reminders — a small card deposit holds the slot; a text confirms the day before
  • First-time colour — a skin test 48 hours ahead, arranged when you book
  • Parking — no High Street meter, no circling Daniel Owen Square on a market day

Made easier

When getting to a salon
is the hard part.

A good many of the people we see stopped going to a salon long before they stopped wanting their hair done properly. The car became a nuisance, or the parking did, or the steps at the door, or simply the business of arranging the whole afternoon around a forty-minute appointment.

Out in the CH7 villages this is doubly true: the bus goes when it goes, and Mold is a long way to travel for a trim. Coming to you removes the entire problem — there is no journey, no waiting room and no lift to arrange.

  • A settled time — morning appointments if that suits better, on a regular cycle you can plan around
  • The same face — the same stylist each visit wherever the diary allows
  • No steps, no stairs — we work in whichever room is easiest for you
  • Company welcome — family or a neighbour sitting in is no trouble at all
  • Unhurried — the appointment is yours; nobody is waiting for the chair

If there is anything that would make a visit easier, say so when you book and we will plan around it.

Questions, answered straight

What people in Mold ask us.

How much does a mobile hairdresser charge in Mold?

A cut and blow-dry starts at £35, a gents’ cut at £18, a faded gents’ cut at £21 and children under 12 at £12. Colour starts at £38 for a root retouch and £75 for balayage — colour prices are for the colour alone, so add a blow-dry from £22 or a cut and blow-dry from £35. There is no travel charge anywhere in the CH7 area — the price you are quoted at booking is the price you pay at the door.

Do you come to my house?

Yes — that is the whole service. Your stylist arrives at the time booked with professional tools, a portable wash station with its own water tanks and salon products, sets up in whichever room suits you, and packs everything away afterwards. All we need is a chair near a plug socket.

Which villages around Mold do you cover?

Thirty-six of them. West of Mold: Gwernaffield, Pantymwyn, Cilcain, Rhydymwyn, Nannerch, Caerwys and Afonwen. Halkyn Mountain: Rhosesmor, Rhes-y-cae, Wern y Gaer, Halkyn, Pentre Halkyn and Brynford. North-east: Sychdyn, Northop, Northop Hall, Mynydd Isa, New Brighton, Bryn-y-Baal and Alltami. East: Buckley, Drury and Padeswood. South: Gwernymynydd, Nercwys, Treuddyn, Leeswood, Coed-talon and Pontblyddyn. And over the tops: Llanferres, Loggerheads, Cadole, Maeshafn, Eryrys and Llanarmon-yn-Iâl.

Do you cover the CH7 postcode?

All of it — CH7 1 through CH7 6, which takes in Mold itself, Buckley and Drury, the Leeswood and Treuddyn villages, the Cilcain and Caerwys side, and the Northop, Sychdyn and Mynydd Isa stretch. The Halkyn Mountain villages just over into CH8 — Halkyn, Pentre Halkyn, Brynford and Rhes-y-cae — are on the round too.

Can you visit someone who finds it hard to get to a salon?

Yes, and it is one of the things a mobile service does best. There are no steps to manage, no waiting room, no car journey and no need to arrange a lift. Appointments can be booked at a settled time of day, with the same stylist each visit wherever the diary allows. We are happy to talk through anything that would make the visit easier before you book.

How far ahead do I need to book colour?

If it is your first colour appointment with us we arrange a skin test 48 hours beforehand, so allow a couple of days. Cuts and blow-dries can usually be fitted in sooner, and regular clients are rebooked before we leave.

Can you do several people in one visit?

Yes. A household visit is one booking, one arrival and one card payment — useful for families, and for households where getting everyone to a salon on the same day is the difficult part.

Do you do wedding hair in the Mold area?

We do. Bridal parties are quoted individually and we travel to the house, the hotel or the venue — Soughton Hall and its Coach House, Northop Hall Country House, Beaufort Park, Highfield Hall and Theatr Clwyd are all inside our normal round.
