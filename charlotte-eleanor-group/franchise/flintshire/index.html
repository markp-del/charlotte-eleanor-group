The territory

Flintshire runs 155,000 people along the Dee — the largest county population in the launch network — threaded through towns the A55 and A494 connect in minutes: Mold, Buckley, Flint, Connah’s Quay, Shotton, Queensferry, Hawarden, Ewloe, Broughton, Holywell. Deeside Industrial Park alone employs thousands who commute past every salon they never have time to sit in.

It’s a working county with working diaries: school runs, shift patterns, home offices. Exactly the market mobile was built for — high-frequency cuts and colour, clustered tightly enough that a route plan barely touches the motorway.

Why Flintshire works

  • Scale — 155,000 residents (ONS Census 2021), the biggest county in the launch four
  • Density — town-to-town drive times among the shortest in North Wales: more chairs, less windscreen
  • The border bonus — the Chester fringe adds premium clientele without adding miles
  • Weak incumbency — local search for mobile hairdressing here returns directories, not businesses; the brand that shows up wins

The maths of a full diary

100–120 clients.
That’s the whole target.

A stabilised full-time column runs on 100–120 recurring clients rebooking on a five-to-six-week cycle — around 25 route-clustered appointments a week. In a territory of this size, that is a sliver of the market: the high street sheds salons every year, and the clients don’t stop needing their hair done. They just stop travelling for it.

Your launch comes with the brand behind it: a wrapped EV on Flintshire’s commuter routes, the group’s local search presence, a launch promotion funded by the marketing levy, and rebooking automation that turns first visits into six-week cycles.
