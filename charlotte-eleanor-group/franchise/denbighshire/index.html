The territory

Denbighshire is where Charlotte Eleanor lives. Home base is St Asaph; the academy that trains every stylist in the network is here; and the county’s 96,000 people split neatly into a populous coast — Rhyl, Prestatyn, Rhuddlan — and a loyal, underserved vale — Denbigh, Ruthin, Llangollen, Corwen.

That mix is a franchisee’s dream diary: volume on the coast, premium loyalty inland, and wedding country all around — Ruthin Castle, Bodelwyddan Castle and Tŷn Dwr Hall are all inside the patch.

Why Denbighshire works

  • Closest support — launch help, cover and supply chain ten minutes away, not a region away
  • Structural at-home demand — 24.7% of residents are 65+ (ONS Census 2021), well above the Wales average
  • Bridal country — castle venues across the vale put the premium ceiling inside the territory
  • First-mover — the first vans on the road launch here; the home county converts fastest

The maths of a full diary

100–120 clients.
That’s the whole target.

A stabilised full-time column runs on 100–120 recurring clients rebooking on a five-to-six-week cycle — around 25 route-clustered appointments a week. In a territory of this size, that is a sliver of the market: the high street sheds salons every year, and the clients don’t stop needing their hair done. They just stop travelling for it.

Your launch comes with the brand behind it: a wrapped EV on Denbighshire’s commuter routes, the group’s local search presence, a launch promotion funded by the marketing levy, and rebooking automation that turns first visits into six-week cycles.
